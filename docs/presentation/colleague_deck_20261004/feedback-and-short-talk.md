# Use the colleague talk to choose the conference story

The purpose is to discover what people understand, remember and want explained.
Do not equate “most positive result” with “most useful conference content.”

Before presenting, invite everyone to note slide numbers rather than interrupt
every unfamiliar term. Take quick clarification questions when needed. At slide 8,
use the planned 20-second show of hands about examples versus the architecture
diagram. At slide 25, allow 45 seconds for individual notes before hearing reactions,
so the first outspoken colleague does not determine everyone's answer.

## Individual response (one minute)

- **Keep:** which slide, example or result would you remember tomorrow, and why?
- **Clarify:** where did you lose the thread? Which term needs an everyday explanation?
- **Move to Q&A:** which slide adds detail without improving the main argument?
- **Scientific concern:** which claim sounds stronger than its evidence? What
  alternative explanation or comparison would you want addressed?
- **One-sentence retelling:** how would you describe what this project learned?

## Discussion prompts

1. Did the concrete input/output examples make the task and its limitations clear?
   In particular, was it clear that a counterfactual target is an instruction,
   and preservation is not the same as correctness?
2. Can you distinguish changing acquisition credit from training the reader with
   PC? Which explanation of PC should remain in the conference talk?
3. Which risk measure changed your view: frequency, conditional severity, maximum,
   expected shortfall, or the analytic probability floor?
4. Was it clear what the active-inference programme still needs? Does choosing
   informative audits make a compelling next experiment?
5. Would an audience at “Active Inference in the Extremes” most benefit from a
   deeper learning-rule explanation, a worked correction, or a deeper tail analysis?
   The final talk must still connect all three themes.

Record responses in a separate dated feedback file; do not silently turn audience
preferences into new experimental claims or commitments.

This is a starting point **after** colleague feedback. It does not replace

| 12 | 24 | 75 | Three takeaways and one open question |

Keep locality/near-miss detail, upper-layer results, cost-accounting detail,
MQuAKE examples, PC controls, the fourth realization and source lists available
in backup. If colleagues strongly favor upper layers or an extra example,
substitute content within this time budget; adding it on top defeats the exercise.

The indispensable qualifications can be short: primary versus exposed populations;
small models; proposed versus implemented active inference; PC here uses autodiff;
finite-range tail evidence; and the per-token/shared-prefix scope of the mixture.
They should travel with the relevant claims, not become a separate administrative
section in the short talk.
