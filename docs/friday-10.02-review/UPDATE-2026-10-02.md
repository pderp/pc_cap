## 2. The analysis of record: HT-17, "what the saved harm distributions support"

Report: `pc_cap/docs/additional_work/HT-17_report.md`; record `pc_cap/logs/additional_work/HT-17/snapshot-20261001-v2/`;
figures `assets/presentation-materials/figures/tails_ht17/snapshot-20261001-v2/{frequency_severity,survival_thresholds}.png`.
Design (Capex's seven points, adopted as written): the observable is Δ = NLL(cap) − NLL(own cap-off) in nats at the
same prefix, on 245,237 ordinary-text positions per cell; the gate's firing indicator is reported separately from
"Δ > 0.01" (a harmful-change indicator); excesses Y = Δ − u over thresholds u ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 0.5, 1} are fitted to the
generalized Pareto and compared with its exponential restriction by held-out-window log-likelihood; uncertainty by
resampling window identities jointly across paired cells (realization, order and seed variation reported separately);
a "not identified" screen (fewer than 100 excesses or 30 windows); fits at the likelihood's endpoint boundary or
outside the manuscript's entropy domain (κ ≤ −1/2 for α = d = 1) are marked invalid, not reported as estimates; no
entropy column. Coverage: 299 cells (225 Stage-4, 30 AW-B memory × arm, 8 reader evaluations, 36 legacy κ-pilot
cells). CPU only, 89 s.

| dataset | cap | harmful-change frequency P(Δ > 0.01) | conditional severity E[Δ ∣ Δ > 0.01], nats | fitted shape at u = 0.01 (per-cell range) | GPD vs exponential, held-out | mean paraphrase retention |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|---|---:|
| zsRE | learned reader v5 | 0.159 % | 1.63 | 0.044–0.059; illustrative-cell interval −0.10 … 0.18 | adds nothing (−0.0004 … +0.0003 nats/excess) | 0.960 |
| zsRE | stable v0 cap | 0.104 % | 1.72 | 0.43–1.03; illustrative interval 0.39–0.78 | GPD better in all 15 cells (+0.15 … +0.59 nats/excess) | 0.186 |
| zsRE | random reader | 0.027 % | 1.81 | below screen | — | 0.524 |
| CounterFact | learned reader v5 | 0.298 % | 1.91 | 0.029–0.098; interval −0.01 … 0.19 | adds nothing (−0.001 … +0.003) | 0.678 |
| CounterFact | stable v0 cap | 0 observed | undefined | — | — | 0.000 |
| CounterFact | random reader | 2.045 % | 3.49 | negative in all cells | 10 of 15 held-out comparisons fail (fitted endpoint excludes a held-out extreme) | 0.121 |

What this supports, in Capex's words: *a difference in finite-range tail behaviour, not three established complexity
classes.* The learned reader's excess distribution is economically described by an exponential on the measured range
(the extra shape parameter buys nothing out of sample; its intervals include zero). The stable v0 cap's zsRE excesses
have a clearly heavier finite-range shape than an exponential predicts, in every cell; above 1 nat thirteen of fifteen
cells fall below the screen, so nothing is said about the farthest tail, and the earlier phrase "infinite fitted
variance" was withdrawn as an extrapolation. The random reader's negative fitted shapes fail out of sample in ten of
fifteen cells: a fitted upper endpoint is not a guarantee about unseen extremes. Frequency and conditional severity
answer different questions and the figure keeps them apart; the learned reader's lower maximum on zsRE coexists with a
higher worst-1 % average than the stable cap.

**The one-nat mixture (AW-B)**, on the ten exposed 300-edit memories: frequency above 0.01 nats unchanged; conditional
severity cut to about one-third, 1.62 → 0.54 nats (zsRE) and 1.93 → 0.58 (CounterFact), paired window intervals
−1.20 … −0.98 and −1.46 … −1.25. Its generalized-Pareto fit hits the endpoint pathology in every memory and gets no
shape estimate. The analytic statement stands on its own: with q = ρ·p_base + (1−ρ)·p_cap and ρ = e⁻¹, q(y) ≥ e⁻¹ p_base(y),
so the loss increase at the same prefix is at most one nat. We now say "we impose a one-nat ceiling on measured
per-position harm", and not that the system changed universality class.

## 3. What changed in the talk

Three wording changes were approved by Charlie on 1 October (decisions DEC-081/081a) and are in the deck
(`pc_cap/docs/presentation/deck_v3/`, export `assets/presentation-materials/deck_v3/round57-delivered/`, rehearsal pack
`…/deck_v3/rehearsal/` with timed 15- and 25-minute scripts):

- **The κ pilot is stated against the manuscript's definition.** What we ran replaced the reader's answer surprisal
  ℓ = −ln p by (1 − e^{−κℓ})/κ for κ = 0.2 and 0.5: a bounded deformation of one loss that saturates at 1/κ and
  down-weights surprising targets. The manuscript's calibrated entropy is a different object (its pointwise factor
  ln_κ(p^{−α/(α+dκ)}) grows with surprisal; it is averaged under the independent-equals weighting; it carries an outer
  1/α root; nothing in the pilot was calibrated to an informational scale). The pilot's null result therefore neither
  confirms nor refutes the coupled entropy or a coupled free energy. The slide says so.
- **Extremes.** Slide 6 now says what the data can and cannot tell us about the shape of the harm, with frequency and
  conditional severity kept apart and the HT-17 figure on screen; no class claim, no W(N) claim; the mixture is a
  proven ceiling. The claim ledger has a new row `HT17-tails` with exactly these limits.
- **Active inference.** One proposed-status paragraph names the manuscript's coupled free energy and pseudo-Markov
  blanket as a candidate mathematical basis for the abstract's "κ-porous blankets", states that applying it requires a
  defined probability model, constraints and a policy loop that we have not specified, and that a boundary in the
  architecture does not by itself demonstrate the conditional independences of a Markov blanket. No equation is
  selected as our objective before the authors clarify the mapping.

Also added to the Stage-4 reports (through their generators, numbers unchanged): the base is GPT-2 small (124M) and

## 5. Groundwork for the collaboration Kenric offered

`pc_cap/docs/additional_work/coupled_objective_note.md` (Capex, CAL-1), with checks in `pc_cap/aw/calibration_demo.py`:

- For the manuscript's one-sided, location-zero, α = d = 1 family, twelve known-distribution checks pass to 2e-11:
  normalisation; the informational-scale condition x f′/f = −1 at x = σ; the escort normaliser Z_q = σ^{−r}/(1+κ) with
  r = κ/(1+κ), q = 1 + r; the escort mean E_{P_q}[X] = σ for every tested κ (0, 0.25, 1, 2) while the ordinary mean
  σ/(1−κ) exists only for κ < 1; the exponential limit; and the calibrated entropy H_κ = 1 + ln_r σ, i.e.
  ((1+κ)σ^{κ/(1+κ)} − 1)/κ. (We read Table 4's subscript the other way at first; direct integration settled it.)
- The note lists the decisions a JAX implementation of a coupled objective for the residual agent would need before
  any training: the modelled random variable and units (a distribution over latent correction states given query and
  memory is the first candidate); the complete generative and variational models including the frozen transformer's
  role and the null action; the explicit coupled free-energy functional with signs, normalisation and constraints
  (substituting a generalized entropy into the ordinary variational formula does not by itself preserve a bound on
  evidence); differentiating through the escort normalisation (the covariance term is not optional); and keeping
  learning separate from policy selection. Minimum evidence before training: pure JAX functions tested against
  independent quadrature, finite-difference gradient checks including the escort term, κ → 0 limits.
- Nothing has been implemented or trained. This is the specification we would like to agree with your group after
  the 15 October talk.

## 6. Questions for Kenric (the ones that matter most, from the longer list in `feedback-MMK-nelson-entropy.md` §6)

1. **The observable.** Is the conditional distribution of positive per-token loss, with the zero/benefit mass reported
   separately, a useful object for the complexity-class framework, or should the coupled model describe predictive
   errors or latent states instead? What would connect that distribution to a state space W(N)? We currently say only
   that we report fitted finite-range behaviour.
2. **Equation 126, positive-κ branch (page 33).** Capex finds that the compensated ratio
   H(W^{1+a})/H(W) · W^{−aκ/(α+dκ)} tends to 1 rather than the printed (1+a)^{1/α}; with α = d = κ = a = 1,
   H(W) = √W − 1 and the ratio is exactly 1 + 1/√W (1.1, 1.01, 1.0001 at W = 10², 10⁴, 10⁸). The κ = 0 branch does give
   (1+a)^{1/α}. Is the secondary scaling exponent intended to be zero for this entropy at κ > 0, or is a different
   limiting prescription meant? (`pc_cap/aw/entropy_feedback_checks.py` reproduces the arithmetic; none of our empirical
   comparisons depend on it.)
3. **Bounded and endpoint-heavy harm.** The mixture's harm has support bounded at one nat and the random reader's fits
   return negative shapes that fail out of sample. How does the framework treat distributions outside the stated
   entropy domain (κ ≤ −1/2 for α = d = 1): compact support with large N_max, truncation, or "not this family"?
4. **For the joint work:** which coupled free energy (physical or informational), over which distribution, with which
   escort weighting and constraints, should be the first JAX objective, and are reference values and gradients
   available from your team's implementation to test against?
